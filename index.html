Lesson 9: Lesson Theme & Title Insights

Tā jīnnián èrshí suì.
He is twenty years old this year.
Tā
Subject (Pronoun)
He
jīnnián
Time Adverbial
this year
èrshí
Numeral
twenty (20)
suì
Measure Word (Age)
years of age
🔍 Title Linguistic & Cultural Insights:
1. Word-for-Word Order: He + this year + twenty + years of age ➔ In Chinese, time expressions ("jīnnián") naturally precede the numerical predicate.
2. Core Grammar Point (Nominal Predicate): Notice there is NO "shì"! In Chinese, sentences stating age, date, price, or time directly use a numeral-measure phrase as the predicate. Adding "shì" (*Tā jīnnián shì èrshí suì) is unnatural and grammatically incorrect.
3. Cultural Note on Age (suì): Traditionally in China, age is counted by lunar new years (xūsuì), where a child is one year old at birth and turns one year older on Chinese New Year. Today, official age (zhōusuì) corresponds directly to the Western birthday calculation.

Expressing Dates: Big to Small Order Pg 141–142

In Chinese, dates proceed strictly from the largest unit of time down to the smallest:

Unit 1: Year
Year (nián)
• Read digit by digit + nián:
yī jiǔ bā èr nián (1982)
èr líng èr liù nián (2026)
Unit 2: Month
Month (yuè)
• Month number (1–12) + yuè:
yīyuè (Jan)
shíyuè (Oct)
shí'èryuè (Dec)
Unit 3: Day
Day (hào / rì)
• Day number (1–31) + hào/rì:
hào (Spoken standard)
rì (Written / formal)
e.g., èrshíqī hào
Unit 4: Weekday
Weekday (xīngqī)
• xīngqī + 1 to 6 / rì / tiān:
xīngqīyī (Mon)
xīngqīsì (Thu)
xīngqīrì / tiān (Sun)
Full Date Sentence Examples:
• Tā yī jiǔ bā èr nián, shíyuè, èrshíqī rì chūshēng. (He was born on October 27, 1982.)
• Jīntiān shì èr líng èr liù nián, shíyuè, èrshíliù hào, xīngqīyī. (Today is Monday, October 26, 2026.)

Asking Hometown vs. Nationality Pg 133
A. Asking Nationality (Strictly Country Level):
Question: Nǐ shì nǎ guó rén?
(Which country are you from?)

Crucial Rule: Because it specifically asks "nǎ guó" (which country), you can ONLY answer with a country name + rén:
• Wǒ shì Zhōngguó rén.
• Wǒ shì Mǎláixīyà rén.
• Wǒ shì Jiānádà rén.
(You cannot answer with a city like Beijing or Sabah here!)
B. Asking Hometown / Origin (Versatile Scope):
Question: Nǐ shì nǎr rén?
(Where are you from / What is your hometown?)

Crucial Rule: "nǎr" means "where" (broad). You can answer with your city or local birthplace, but abroad it can also naturally be answered with your country:
• City / Hometown: Wǒ shì Běijīng rén / Shābā rén.
• Country (Abroad): Wǒ shì Zhōngguó rén / Mǎláixīyà rén.
Usage & Constraints of "cānjiā" Pg 133
• Core Pragmatic Meaning (Voluntary & Elective Participation):
"cānjiā" naturally denotes voluntary, proactive, or elective participation in social events, parties, celebrations, sports matches, or organized competitions.
✔ cānjiā jùhuì (voluntarily attend a party/gathering)
✔ cānjiā bǐsài (voluntarily participate in a tournament/match)
⚠️ Essential Classroom Distinction (Institutional Attendance vs. Elective Join):
Social gatherings, celebrations, and sports events are voluntary activities you proactively choose to join. In contrast, regular academic classes are institutional curricular schedules rather than voluntary recreational events.
Therefore, Chinese strictly uses shàngkè (to attend class) instead of *cānjiā:
✔ shàngkè / shàng Hànyǔ kè (attend / take Chinese class)
✘ cānjiā Hànyǔ kè (Grammatically and pragmatically incorrect!)

A: Jīntiān shì shíyuè èrshíqī hào. (Today is October 27th.)

A: Wǒ de shēngri shì bāyuè èrshíyī hào. (My birthday is August 21st.)

Q5: Nǐ shénme shíhou yǒu kè? (When do you have class?)

A: Wǒ shàngwǔ jiǔ diǎn yǒu kè. (I have class at 9 o'clock in the morning.)
Q7: Tā jīnnián duō dà? (How old is he this year? - Adults / General)
A: Tā jīnnián èrshí suì. (He is 20 years old this year.)
Q8: Nǐ dìdi jīnnián jǐ suì? (How old is your younger brother? - Children under 12)
A: Tā jīnnián sān suì. (He is 3 years old this year.)
Q9: Nǐ shǔ shénme? (What is your zodiac sign?)
A: Wǒ shǔ gǒu. (I was born in the year of the Dog.)
Q10: Tā shì nǎr rén? (Where is he from?)
A: Tā shì Běijīng rén. (He is from Beijing.)

Time Clause Structure: "... de shíhou" Pg 143

Plain English Explanation:
Simply put: Just put de shíhou right after any action to mean "When doing [action]"!
For example: chīfàn (eat) + de shíhou = chīfàn de shíhou (When eating).
In a complete sentence, place it right after the Subject and before what happens next:

SubjectSubject
+
[Action / Event] + de shíhou
➔
Main ActionMain Action / Predicate
• Wǒ chīfàn de shíhou bú kàn shū. (I do not read books when eating.)
• Wǒ chī hànbǎo de shíhou yào hē kělè. (I want to drink cola when eating a hamburger.)
• Zhōngguó rén guò shēngri de shíhou chī dàngāo ma? (Do Chinese people eat cake when celebrating birthdays?)
Making Proposals: "... hǎo ma?" Pg 143
Wǒ mǎi yí ge dà dàngāo, hǎo ma?

Affirmative Answers: Hǎo a. / Hǎo. / Tài hǎo le.
Polite Refusal: Bú yòng le. / Duìbuqǐ, wǒ méiyǒu shíjiān.

Text 1: Making an Appointment (Pg 130–131)

🎯 Communicative Focus
1. Making an appointment: Inquiring whether someone is busy (máng bu máng), checking class schedules (shàngwǔ / xiàwǔ yǒu kè), and agreeing on a free day (xīngqīrì).
2. Inquiring about someone's age and birthplace: Finding out a friend's birthday (shēngri), asking age (duō dà), identifying their zodiac sign (shǔ gǒu), and inquiring about their hometown (Běijīng rén).

• Shíyīyuè shí'èr hào wǒmen zài lái chī shòumiàn. (On November 12th, we will come again to eat longevity noodles.)
🍜 Culture Note: Longevity Noodles (shòumiàn)

Eating long noodles on birthdays is a traditional Chinese custom representing long life ("longevity"). In daily life, "shòumiàn" can also simply be referred to as "miàn" (noodles).

🌟 Core Grammar Summary
⏳ The Temporal Pyramid
• Hierarchy: Strict sequence from Largest ➔ Smallest:
Year ➔ Month ➔ Day ➔ Weekday ➔ Time of Day
• Position: Time words function as adverbials and must always sit before the verb (e.g., Wǒ jīntiān hěn máng, never at the very end).
🎂 Nominal Predicates: NO "shì"
• Direct Numerical Predicate: Stating age and dates requires NO copula "shì":
✔ Tā jīnnián èrshí suì.
✘ Tā jīnnián shì èrshí suì.
• Age Questions: Use duō dà for adults and peers; use jǐ suì strictly for children under 12.
🌏 Country vs. Hometown
• Nationality (Strict Country Level):
Nǐ shì nǎ guó rén? ➔ Country ONLY (Zhōngguó rén / Mǎláixīyà rén).
• Hometown / Origin (Versatile Scope):
Nǐ shì nǎr rén? ➔ City or hometown (Běijīng rén / Shābā rén), or country when traveling abroad.
🙋 Social Etiquette & Planning
• Curriculum vs. Voluntary: Use shàngkè for academic classes; use cānjiā only for voluntary parties and competitions.
• Time Clauses: Place ... de shíhou right after an action to mean "when doing...".
• Proposals & Futures: Suggest plans with ... hǎo ma? and anticipate meeting again with zài + Verb (zài lái chī).
