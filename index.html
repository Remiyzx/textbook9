Grammar: Dates Order, Nominal Predicate & Origin (Pg 141-143)
1. Expressing Dates: Big to Small Order (Pg 141–142):
YearYear (nián)
➔
MonthMonth (yuè)
➔
DayDay (hào / rì)
➔
WeekdayWeekday (xīngqī)
Reading Rules & Examples:
• Year numbers are read digit by digit: yī jiǔ bā èr nián (1982).
• Month numbers + yuè: shíyuè (October).
• Day numbers + hào (spoken) / rì (written): èrshíqī hào / èrshíqī rì.
• Full Date Example: yī jiǔ bā èr nián shíyuè èrshíqī rì, xīngqīrì.
2. Asking Age Rules & Nominal Predicate (Pg 133, 143):
Adults & Peers:
Nǐ jīnnián duō dà?
Children (Under 10):
Nǐ jīnnián jǐ suì?
✔ Tā jīnnián èrshí suì. (Nominal predicate - NO "shì" needed!)
✘ Tā jīnnián shì èrshí suì.
3. Asking Hometown vs Nationality & "cānjiā" Rules:
A. Asking Hometown / Place of Birth: Tā shì nǎr rén? ➔ Tā shì Běijīng rén.
B. Asking Nationality: Tā shì nǎ guó rén? ➔ Tā shì Zhōngguó rén.
C. "cānjiā" Constraints: ✔ cānjiā jùhuì | ✘ cānjiā Hànyǔ kè (Use shàng kè instead).
🎯 Key Q&A Reinforcement: Asking Dates & Personal Details
Q1: Tā nǎ nián chūshēng? (Which year was he born?)
A: Tā yī jiǔ bā èr nián chūshēng. (He was born in 1982.)
Q2: Jīntiān shì jǐ yuè jǐ hào? (What date is today?)
A: Jīntiān shì shíyuè èrshíqī hào. (Today is October 27th.)
Q3: Nǐ de shēngri shì nǎ tiān? (When/which day is your birthday?)
A: Wǒ de shēngri shì bāyuè èrshíyī hào. (My birthday is August 21st.)
Q4: Míngtiān shì xīngqī jǐ? (What day of the week is tomorrow?)
A: Míngtiān shì xīngqīsì. (Tomorrow is Thursday.)
Q5: Nǐ shénme shíhou yǒu kè? (When do you have class?)
A: Wǒ míngtiān shàngwǔ yǒu kè. (I have class tomorrow morning.)
Q6: Nǐ shàngwǔ jǐ diǎn yǒu kè? (What time do you have class in the morning?)
A: Wǒ shàngwǔ jiǔ diǎn yǒu kè. (I have class at 9 o'clock in the morning.)
Q7: Tā jīnnián duō dà? (How old is he this year? - Adults / General)
A: Tā jīnnián èrshí suì. (He is 20 years old this year.)
Q8: Nǐ dìdi jīnnián jǐ suì? (How old is your younger brother? - Children under 10)
A: Tā jīnnián sān suì. (He is 3 years old this year.)
Q9: Tā shì nǎr rén? (Where is he from?)
A: Tā shì Běijīng rén. (He is from Beijing.)
Q10: Nǐ shǔ shénme? (What is your zodiac sign?)
A: Wǒ shǔ gǒu. (I was born in the year of the Dog.)

Grammar: Time Clauses & Proposals (Pg 143)
1. Time Clause Structure: "... de shíhou" (When / While):
• Zhōngguó rén guò shēngri de shíhou chī dàngāo ma? (Do Chinese people eat cake when celebrating birthdays?)
• Chīfàn de shíhou, wǒ bú kàn shū. (When eating, I don't read books.)
2. Suggestion Structure: "... hǎo ma?" (Pg 143):
Wǒ mǎi yí ge dà dàngāo, hǎo ma?

Affirmative Answers: Hǎo a. / Hǎo. / Tài hǎo le.
Polite Refusal: Bú yòng le. / Duìbuqǐ, wǒ méiyǒu shíjiān.

Text 1: Dialogue Lines 1–17
1. (Lin Na, how are you? Are you busy?)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Lín Nà, nǐ zěnmeyàng? Máng bu máng?
2. (I am very busy today.)
Lín Nà: Wǒ jīntiān hěn máng.
3. (Do you have class tomorrow morning?)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Míngtiān shàngwǔ nǐ yǒu méiyǒu kè?
4. (What day is tomorrow?)
Lín Nà: Míngtiān shì xīngqī jǐ?
5. (Tomorrow is Thursday.)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Míngtiān shì xīngqīsì.
6. (I have class both in the morning and afternoon.)
Lín Nà: Wǒ shàngwǔ, xiàwǔ dōu yǒu kè.
7. (Do you have time on Sunday?)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Nǐ xīngqīrì yǒu shíjiān ma?
8. (What date is Sunday?)
Lín Nà: Xīngqīrì shì jǐ hào?
9. (Sunday is October 27th, it is Song Hua's birthday.)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Xīngqīrì shì shíyuè èrshíqī hào, shì Sòng Huá de shēngri.
10. (Is that so? How old is he this year?)
Lín Nà: Shì ma? Tā jīnnián duō dà?
11. (Song Hua was born on October 27, 1982, his zodiac is dog. He is twenty years old this year.)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Sòng Huá yī jiǔ bā èr nián shíyuè èrshíqī rì chūshēng, shǔ gǒu. Tā jīnnián èrshí suì.
12. (Where is he from?)
Lín Nà: Tā shì nǎr rén?
13. (He is a Beijinger. Both his father and mother are in Beijing. We have a party on Sunday afternoon to celebrate his birthday. Libo and Dawei are both going, will you participate?)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Tā shì Běijīng rén. Tā bàba, māma dōu zài Běijīng. Xīngqīrì xiàwǔ wǒmen yǒu yí ge jùhuì, zhùhè tā de shēngri. Lìbō, Dàwéi dōu qù, nǐ cānjiā bu cānjiā?
14. (That's great! Of course I will participate. Do Chinese people eat cake when celebrating birthdays?)
Lín Nà: Tài hǎo le! Wǒ dāngrán cānjiā. Zhōngguó rén guò shēngri de shíhou chī dàngāo ma?
15. (Yes, eat cake.)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Chī dàngāo.
16. (I'll buy a big cake, okay?)
Lín Nà: Wǒ mǎi yí ge dà dàngāo, hǎo ma?
17. (Okay. I'll buy two bottles of red wine.)
Wáng Xiǎoyún: Hǎo a. Wǒ mǎi liǎng píng hóng pútaojiǔ.

Grammar: Greetings & Emotional Expressions
1. "zhù" vs "zhùhè":
zhù (Wish):
Directly wishing someone well (casual/everyday):
Zhù nǐ shēngri kuàilè!
zhùhè (Congratulate):
Formal congratulation on an event:
zhùhè tā de shēngri
2. "kuàilè" vs "gāoxìng":
kuàilè (Happy / Festive):
Long-term happiness or festive wishes:
shēngri kuàilè
gāoxìng (Glad / Pleased):
Instant feeling / mood right now:
Nǐmen lái, wǒ hěn gāoxìng.

🍜 Culture Note: Longevity Noodles (shòumiàn)

Eating long noodles on birthdays is a traditional Chinese custom representing long life ("longevity").
